Lab 1: AI Vision

Make AI see and understand the world around you.

Activity 1 — Google Lens

🌿 Visual Search & Identification

Point your camera at anything and AI identifies it instantly — plants, products, landmarks, animals, math problems.

  1. Open Google Lens (in the Google app on your phone, or the search bar on Android)
  2. Point your camera at a plant, insect, building, sneaker, or food
  3. See instant results — species name, product matches, reviews, history
  4. Try pointing at a math equation — it will solve it!
  5. Try pointing at text in another language — it'll translate it live on screen
→ Open Google Lens
💡 What makes it different: Google Lens is built for instant visual answers — no typing needed. Just point and go.

Activity 2 — ChatGPT equivalent

📸 Photo Detective (ChatGPT Vision)

ChatGPT can also see images — but you can have a full conversation about what it sees.

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone (chat.openai.com)
  2. Tap the camera/photo icon in the message bar
  3. Take a photo of something interesting
  4. Ask: "What do you see? Describe it in detail."
  5. Follow up: "How much does this cost?" or "What year was this made?"
→ Try with ChatGPT
💡 Key difference: Google Lens gives instant facts. ChatGPT lets you have a back-and-forth conversation about what it sees.

Lab 2: AI Image Generation

Create images that have never existed before — from just words.

Activity 1 — Google Gemini image generation

🖌️ AI Image Creator with Gemini

Gemini can generate images from text prompts in supported regions/accounts. Just describe what you want and refine the result in chat.

  1. Go to gemini.google.com (sign in with your Google account)
  2. Type: "Generate an image of..." followed by your description
  3. Be specific! Include style, colors, mood, setting, camera angle
  4. If image generation is available for your account, Gemini creates the image in seconds
  5. Ask for variations: "Make it more colorful" or "Try it as pixel art"

Example prompts to try:

  • "A golden retriever astronaut on the moon, photorealistic"
  • "A cozy coffee shop in Tokyo during rain, anime style"
  • "A sneaker designed by nature — made of moss, flowers, and vines"
  • "A basketball court floating in the clouds at sunset, cinematic"
  • "A dragon playing basketball in a school gym, digital art"
→ Open Google Gemini
💡 What makes it different: Gemini image generation is built into Google's AI assistant, so you can create and revise images conversationally. Availability and limits vary by account, region, and plan.

Activity 2 — ChatGPT image generation

🎭 Conversational Image Creation

ChatGPT image generation also lets you create and edit images conversationally.

  1. Open ChatGPT
  2. Type: "Generate an image of a wolf wearing a hoodie and headphones, cyberpunk city, digital art"
  3. Don't like something? Say: "Make the background more colorful" or "Change it to anime style"
  4. Keep refining — AI remembers your whole conversation
→ Try with ChatGPT
💡 Key difference: Both Gemini and ChatGPT support conversational image generation. The exact model names and limits change over time, so focus on the skill: write a clear prompt, inspect the result, then refine.

Lab 3: AI Audio & Voice

Talk to AI, translate in real-time, and create with your voice.

Activity 1 — ElevenLabs

🗣️ AI Voice Cloning & Text-to-Speech

Type any text and hear it spoken in realistic AI voices. Some voice cloning features are limited or paid, and should only be used with permission.

  1. Go to elevenlabs.io and create a free account
  2. Click "Text to Speech" in the sidebar
  3. Type a sentence and pick a voice — try the different options!
  4. Hit "Generate" and listen to how realistic it sounds
  5. Bonus: Explore voice cloning only with your own voice and permission; full cloning features may require a paid plan
→ Open ElevenLabs
💡 What makes it different: ElevenLabs specializes in ultra-realistic voice AI. The voices sound almost indistinguishable from real people — which is cool but also why you should think critically about audio you hear online!

Activity 2 — Google Translate

🌍 Real-Time Voice Translation

Speak in one language and hear/read it back in another — instantly.

  1. Open Google Translate (translate.google.com)
  2. Tap the microphone icon
  3. Speak a sentence in English
  4. Watch it translate to Spanish, French, Japanese — anything!
  5. Tap the speaker icon to hear the translation spoken aloud
→ Open Google Translate

Activity 3 — ChatGPT equivalent

🎙️ Voice Conversation (ChatGPT Voice)

ChatGPT has a voice mode too — have a full back-and-forth conversation with AI using just your voice.

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone
  2. Tap the headphone icon (Voice mode)
  3. Talk naturally — ask questions, get explanations, practice presentations
  4. Interrupt it anytime — it'll adjust mid-sentence
→ Try with ChatGPT
💡 Key difference: ElevenLabs is best for creating voice content (narration, cloning). ChatGPT Voice is best for interactive conversations.

Lab 4: AI Document Reading (OCR)

AI can read handwriting, scan documents, and extract text from images.

Activity 1 — Adobe Scan

📋 Pocket Document Scanner

Adobe Scan turns your phone into a professional document scanner — with AI that auto-crops, enhances, and reads text.

  1. Download Adobe Scan (free on iOS and Android)
  2. Point your camera at a document, whiteboard, receipt, or book page
  3. It auto-detects edges and scans it perfectly — no manual cropping
  4. Tap the scan — all text is now searchable and copy-paste ready
  5. Try scanning your handwritten notes — it can read handwriting too!
→ Get Adobe Scan (Free)
💡 What makes it different: Adobe Scan is purpose-built for document scanning — auto-crop, perspective correction, OCR built in. Great for school notes and worksheets.

Activity 2 — ChatGPT equivalent

✍️ Conversational OCR (ChatGPT Vision)

ChatGPT can also read images — and then you can ask follow-up questions about what it read.

  1. Open ChatGPT and tap the camera icon
  2. Take a photo of handwriting, a printed page, or a whiteboard
  3. Ask: "Type out all the text in this image"
  4. Then ask: "Summarize this" or "Translate this to Spanish" or "Is this correct?"
→ Try with ChatGPT
💡 Key difference: Adobe Scan is best for clean document scanning and PDFs. ChatGPT lets you ask questions about what it reads and do more with it (summarize, translate, correct).

Lab 5: AI Music Creation

Create original songs with AI — just describe the vibe.

Activity 1

🎤 Your First AI Song

Describe a song and AI will compose it — lyrics, melody, instruments, everything.

  1. Go to suno.com and create a free account
  2. Click "Create" and describe your song
  3. Example: "An upbeat pop song about being done with homework on a Friday"
  4. Wait 30 seconds — AI generates a full song with vocals!
  5. Try different genres: lo-fi, rap, country, jazz, rock

Prompt ideas:

  • "A chill lo-fi beat for studying late at night"
  • "An epic rap battle between a cat and a dog"
  • "A country song about losing your WiFi connection"
  • "A motivational anthem for waking up early"
→ Open Suno
💡 Challenge: Create a theme song for yourself or your friend group. Share it and see who makes the best one!

Lab 6: AI Code Writing

Build websites and games by telling AI what you want — no coding experience needed.

Activity 1 — GitHub Copilot

🤖 Build Anything with GitHub Copilot

GitHub Copilot is an AI coding tool used by many developers. Student access is available through GitHub Education for eligible verified students, but you should check current student access because sign-up availability can change.

  1. Sign up at github.com (free)
  2. Apply for the GitHub Student Developer Pack and check current Copilot Student eligibility
  3. Install VS Code (free code editor) and add the GitHub Copilot extension
  4. Open the Copilot Chat panel and type: "Build me a trivia quiz game in HTML with a score counter and timer"
  5. Copilot can help generate the game code — review it, then click "Apply" to put it in your file
  6. Open the file in your browser — your game works!
  7. Ask Copilot to change things: "Add confetti when you win" or "Make it about anime"

Ideas to build:

  • "Build me a whack-a-mole game with emojis and a high score"
  • "Create a personal website about basketball with a dark theme"
  • "Make a reaction time tester with colorful animations"
  • "Build a to-do list app with local storage"
  • "Create a typing speed test that tracks WPM"
→ Check GitHub Education Access
💡 What makes it different: GitHub Copilot works inside a code editor, suggests code as you type, and can use project context — not just one message at a time.

Activity 2 — Claude

🌐 Build a Website Draft with Claude

Claude can help generate a complete single-file website from a plain-English prompt. You can copy the code into a local HTML file and open it in your browser.

  1. Go to claude.ai and sign in or create an account
  2. Ask: "Create a single-file HTML website for a student AI workshop. Include sections for what AI is, tools to try, and a challenge."
  3. Copy the code into a file named index.html
  4. Open that file in your browser
  5. Ask Claude to improve it: "Make it more colorful" or "Add a section for prompt tips"
→ Open Claude
💡 Key difference: GitHub Copilot works inside your code editor with project context. Claude is useful for quickly drafting a single-file website or explaining code in a chat.

Activity 3 — ChatGPT equivalent

🌐 Quick Code with ChatGPT

ChatGPT can also write full websites and games — you just copy-paste the code into a file.

  1. Open ChatGPT
  2. Type: "Build me a [game/website] in a single HTML file that I can open in my browser"
  3. Copy the code, save as index.html, open in browser
  4. Ask follow-ups: "Add dark mode" or "Make it responsive"
→ Try with ChatGPT
